# Precedent Log

Running log of edge-case decisions and the reasoning behind them. When a similar case arises, reviewers should reference existing precedents rather than starting from scratch.

## Format

For each entry, record:

- **Date**: When the decision was made
- **Submission ID**: The tracking ID (e.g. GSF-SUB-2026-0003)
- **Issue**: What was novel or ambiguous
- **Decision**: What the reviewer/committee decided
- **Reasoning**: Why, and how it should apply to future similar cases

## Entries

- **Date**: 2026-07-27
- **Submission ID**: GSF-SUB-2026-0002 (Offlyn.ai — Offlyn Clipper)
- **Issue**: The submission's "Score and period" item reports a single 60-minute benchmark measured once, on one device, on one day (R = 1), rather than a score aggregated over an extended real-world operating period. The FAQ notes that "very short periods (e.g., one day) may not capture representative patterns" and asks applicants to state and justify their choice of period.
- **Decision**: Accepted as meeting the completeness bar for Item 2. The applicant clearly disclosed the single-benchmark nature of the measurement (device, method, duration) and did not represent it as reflective of broader production usage.
- **Reasoning**: Per the program's design, review checks disclosure completeness and transparency — not whether the underlying methodology or sample size is the best available or statistically representative. A single controlled benchmark, clearly labeled and methodologically disclosed, satisfies that bar. Future single-instance or consumer-device benchmark submissions should be evaluated the same way: check that the limited scope of measurement is disclosed and not overstated, rather than requiring a minimum sample size or measurement duration. That said, this may be worth revisiting as an explicit guidance update for consumer-device submissions specifically — flagged for team discussion, not a settled program requirement change.
